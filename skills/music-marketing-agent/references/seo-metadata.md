# SEO and Metadata

Discovery through search - the part of music marketing that keeps working after the
posting stops.

## Metadata (fix before release - it is hard to change after)

Wrong metadata costs royalties and splits an artist's catalog across duplicate profiles.

- **Artist name spelled identically everywhere.** One variant creates a second Spotify
  profile and splits the listener base permanently
- **Title casing consistent** across all platforms
- **Features in the title field** as `Song (feat. Artist)` - not in the artist field.
  This is what makes the track appear on the featured artist's profile
- **Version tags in parentheses:** `(Acoustic)`, `(Sped Up)`, `(Remix)`, `(Live)`. These
  are searched terms and they prevent duplicate-detection problems
- **Genre and mood** set accurately - this feeds algorithmic recommendation. Overreaching
  into a bigger genre puts the track in worse company and hurts it
- **ISRC** unique per recording; a new ISRC for each version
- **Writer, producer, and publishing splits registered** before release, with a PRO and
  a publishing administrator. Unregistered splits are how independent artists lose money
  quietly for years
- **Language and explicit flags** correct - a wrong explicit flag can block playlist
  eligibility

## Keyword layers

Build a keyword set per release and reuse it across YouTube, Pinterest, the site, and
descriptions:

| Layer | Example shape |
|---|---|
| Artist | `[artist name]`, `[artist] new song`, `[artist] [song]` |
| Song | `[song] lyrics`, `[song] official video`, `[song] live` |
| Genre | `[subgenre] 2026`, `underground [genre]` |
| Mood / use | `[genre] for driving at night`, `sad [genre] songs` |
| Similar-artist | `songs like [artist]`, `artists similar to [artist]` |
| Local | `[city] [genre] artist`, `[genre] out of [city]` |

**Mood and use-case keywords are the most winnable.** An unknown artist cannot outrank
anyone for a genre term, but "songs for late night drives 2026" is reachable, and the
listener intent behind it is high.

**Local keywords are the second most winnable** and they build the scene relationships
that lead to shows and collaborations.

## YouTube

Title:
- Under 60 characters
- Searchable term first: `Song Title - Artist (Official Video)`
- For non-music uploads, lead with what someone would type: `How I Made [Song] - Full Beat Breakdown`
- No clickbait the video does not pay off; it tanks retention, which is the metric that
  actually governs distribution

Description:
- **First two lines carry the weight** - that is all that shows before "more," and it is
  weighted for search. Put the song, artist, and one hook line there
- Then: streaming links, social links, timestamps, full credits, lyrics
- Lyrics in the description is free long-tail search volume - people search lines
- 3-5 relevant hashtags at the end

Tags: 10-15, mixing exact title, artist name, genre, mood, and similar artists.

Other levers: accurate closed captions (indexed, and they widen the audience), custom
thumbnails on everything, playlists per release, end screens and cards to the next video,
and pinned comments carrying the stream link.

## Google indexing

- Claim the Google Knowledge Panel for the artist
- A real website with an indexable domain - not only social profiles. Socials are rented;
  a domain is owned
- Schema.org `MusicGroup` and `MusicRecording` markup on the site
- A lyrics page per song - durable long-tail search traffic
- Consistent name, bio, and image across every profile so entity resolution links them
- Wikidata entry if the artist meets notability requirements; do not fabricate notability

## Website

Minimum viable, in priority order:

1. **Email capture above the fold** with a real reason to sign up - unreleased demo,
   early access, first look at the video
2. Streaming links to every platform
3. Lyrics pages (search traffic)
4. Press kit: bio, photos, links, contact
5. Tour dates if any
6. Blog or notes if the artist will actually maintain one - abandoned blogs read worse
   than none

## Email list

The only audience nobody can take away, and the highest-converting channel at release.

- Ask on every platform, repeatedly. Most people need several exposures
- Give something real for the signup
- Send at least twice a month so the list stays warm - a cold list opened once a year
  performs like no list
- Segment by engagement once it is big enough to matter
- Release day: email first, before the social posts
- Always: real consent, working unsubscribe, physical address (CAN-SPAM)
