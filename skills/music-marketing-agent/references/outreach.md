# Outreach

Playlist curators, blogs, podcasts, DJs, influencers, collaborators.

Outreach is where artists most often get scammed, get their account limited, or get a
track flagged. The rules below are protective, not decorative.

## Non-negotiable rules

1. **Never pay for placement.** Paying a curator for a playlist add violates Spotify's
   terms and is a fast route to fraud flags on the track. Paying for *promotion services*
   that guarantee streams or placements is the same thing wearing a different name.
   Legitimate paid options exist - advertising, a real publicist, Spotify Marquee /
   Discovery Mode (understanding Discovery Mode takes a royalty cut) - and those are ads,
   not placements.
2. **Never send the same message twice.** Every message needs a line that could only have
   been written for that recipient. Mass-identical DMs are spam, get accounts restricted,
   and permanently burn curator relationships in a small world.
3. **Follow the stated submission method.** If a curator says "submissions via the form
   only," the form is the only correct channel. DMing anyway is an instant no.
4. **One follow-up maximum**, 7-10 days later, short. Then stop. Silence is an answer.
5. **Never scrape and blast personal email addresses.** Use published submission
   addresses and forms.
6. **Do not fabricate contacts.** If a curator, blog, or handle cannot be verified as
   real and currently active, describe the type of target and where to look instead. A
   made-up contact wastes the artist's time and may point them at an uninvolved stranger.

## Spotting a fake curator

The playlist submission economy is full of scams. Before pitching, check:

| Signal | Read |
|---|---|
| Asks for money for a "guaranteed" placement | Scam. Walk away |
| Follower count high, but track saves near zero | Bot-inflated playlist. A placement here can get the *artist* flagged |
| Playlist mixes unrelated genres with no coherence | Farm playlist |
| Hundreds of tracks, all added the same week | Farm playlist |
| No listener engagement, no curator identity, no social presence | Not a real audience |
| Promises a specific stream number | Nobody can promise this legitimately |

A placement on a bot-inflated playlist is worse than no placement: the fraudulent streams
attach to the artist's track and the artist absorbs the penalty.

**Good targets:** 1k-50k follower playlists with real save rates, run by an identifiable
person, updated regularly, genre-coherent. Small and real beats large and fake every time.

## Where to find real targets

- **Spotify for Artists** - the editorial pitch. Free, and the highest-value single
  submission available. At least 7 days before release, ideally 4 weeks
- **"Fans also like"** on similar artists' profiles - then look at what playlists those
  artists appear on
- Blog aggregators and genre blogs that publish submission guidelines
- Podcasts that feature independent music in the genre
- Local radio, college radio, and community stations - genuinely reachable, and they
  matter more for local scene-building than most artists assume
- DJs and selectors playing the genre in the artist's city
- Submission platforms (SubmitHub, Groover, Musosoup) - these charge for *guaranteed
  listening and feedback*, not for placement. That distinction is legitimate, but the
  economics are usually poor for an artist with a small budget; treat as optional

## Templates

Adapt every one of these. Sending them verbatim defeats the purpose.

### Playlist curator

```
Subject: [Genre] submission - "[Song]" by [Artist]

Hi [Name],

I found [Playlist Name] through [specific, true route]. [One specific, accurate
observation - a track on it, the sequencing, a recent addition.]

I make [genre] out of [city]. My new single "[Song]" is out [date] - [one line on what
it actually is, no adjective pileup]. It sits close to [artist on their playlist], so I
thought it might fit.

Private link: [link]
Spotify: [link]

No worries either way - thanks for running the playlist.

[Artist]
[socials]
```

### Blog / press

```
Subject: [Artist] - "[Song]" [genre] // out [date]

Hi [Name],

[Specific reference to something they actually wrote, and why it connected.]

I'm [Artist], [genre] from [city]. "[Song]" releases [date]. [One sentence of story -
the reason the song exists, not a description of the genre.]

Press kit: [link]  Stream: [link]  Photos: [link]

Happy to send anything else you need.

[Artist]
```

### Podcast

```
Subject: Guest / music feature - [Artist]

Hi [Name],

I listened to the [specific episode] with [guest] - [genuine specific reaction].

I'm [Artist], [genre] from [city]. [One line on the story that makes for a good
conversation - not the release itself.] I could talk about [specific angle relevant to
their show].

Music: [link]

Either way, appreciate the show.

[Artist]
```

### DJ

```
Hi [Name],

Caught your set at [venue/stream] - [specific track they played or a specific moment].

I make [genre] out of [city]. New one, "[Song]," is [tempo]bpm in [key] - might sit well
next to [artist they play].

Download / stream: [link]

Cheers,
[Artist]
```

### Creator collaboration

```
Hi [Name],

[Specific video of theirs, and what actually worked about it.]

I'm [Artist] - I make [genre]. I have a track, "[Song]," that I think fits what you do
with [specific element of their content].

Idea: [concrete format - duet, they use the sound for X, a remix of their audio.]
Happy to send the audio early if you want first use of it.

[Artist]
```

## Tracking

Keep a table. Without it, artists double-pitch the same curator and look careless.

| Date | Target | Type | Channel | Personalization used | Reply | Result | Follow-up due |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

**Realistic expectations:** 5-15% reply rate on well-personalized outreach; 1-5% on
generic. Most placements come from the second or third release someone has seen from
the artist, not the first. Outreach compounds - the goal of a first pitch is often just
to be recognized on the next one.
